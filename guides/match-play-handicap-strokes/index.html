Match play handicap strokes explained

Who gets strokes in a net match, how many, and on which holes. For a 1vs1 and for a team match.

By Robbie, founder of Parmate | Updated 28 November 2026

The short answer

  1. Work out each player’s course handicap for the tee they play. In singles match play the WHS allowance is 100%, so this is also the playing handicap.
  2. The player with the lowest playing handicap plays off zero.
  3. The other player gets the difference in strokes.
  4. The strokes fall on the holes with stroke index 1, 2, 3 and so on. A difference above 18 gives a second stroke, again from stroke index 1.

Example: a 1vs1 match

Player A has index 8.2 and player B has index 22.4. They play the same tee: course rating 70.2, slope 122, par 71.

  • A: 8.2 × 122 ÷ 113 − 0.8 = 8.05, so 8.
  • B: 22.4 × 122 ÷ 113 − 0.8 = 23.38, so 23.

A plays off zero. B gets 23 − 8 = 15 strokes: one on each of the holes with stroke index 1 to 15. On those holes B’s score counts one lower. The lower net score wins the hole.

Where the stroke index comes in

Every hole has a stroke index from 1 to 18. Stroke index 1 is where higher handicap golfers need the most help. Strokes are handed out in that order. In a 9 hole match the same order is used across the 9 holes you play.

Different tees

The course handicap already includes course rating minus par, so players on different tees, or men and women, can play a fair match. Work out each course handicap for that player’s own tee and compare.

A team match

When two scramble teams play a match, first work out each team handicap with the scramble allowance (35/15 for teams of two). Then the same rule applies: the team with the lowest team handicap plays off zero and the other team gets the difference, on the holes in stroke index order. This is how Parmate counts holes in a Team Match.

Gross match play

You can also play without handicap strokes, simply the lowest score on each hole. In Parmate this is the gross option of a 1vs1 Match.

All square after 18

If the match is level at the end, players or the committee decide: play extra holes until one side wins a hole, or call it a halved match. Parmate lets you choose either.

How the score is told

Match play counts holes, not strokes. "2 up" means one side has won two more holes. "3 and 2" means a side is 3 holes up with only 2 to play, so the match is over.

Questions

What is the handicap allowance for singles match play?

100% of the course handicap in the WHS recommendations. The lower player plays off zero and the other gets the full difference.

What if the difference is more than 18 strokes?

Then the player gets one stroke on every hole and a second stroke on the holes with the lowest stroke indexes, starting again from stroke index 1.
