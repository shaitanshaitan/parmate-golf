Course handicap explained

How your handicap index becomes the number of strokes you get on a particular course and tee, for 18 and for 9 holes.

By Robbie, founder of Parmate | Updated 28 November 2026

The short answer

Course handicap = handicap index × (slope ÷ 113) + (course rating − par)

For 9 holes, use half your handicap index and the 9 hole course rating, slope and par. The result is your course handicap for that tee. In competitions a handicap allowance may then turn it into a playing handicap.

The four numbers you need

  • Handicap index: your general playing level, kept up to date by your club or federation.
  • Slope rating: how much harder the course is for a higher handicap golfer than for a scratch golfer. 113 is the standard; most courses sit between about 110 and 140.
  • Course rating: what a scratch golfer is expected to score from that tee.
  • Par: the par of the holes you play from that tee.

You find slope, course rating and par on the scorecard, the handicap table in the clubhouse or the club website, separate for men and women and for every tee.

Worked example, 18 holes

Handicap index 14.5, tee with slope 122, course rating 70.2 and par 71:

14.5 × (122 ÷ 113) + (70.2 − 71) = 15.65 − 0.8 = 14.85

Rounded, the course handicap is 15.

Worked example, 9 holes

Same player, a 9 hole loop with slope 119, course rating 33.0 and par 34:

(14.5 ÷ 2) × (119 ÷ 113) + (33.0 − 34) = 7.64 − 1 = 6.63

Rounded, the 9 hole course handicap is 7.

Course handicap and playing handicap

The course handicap is what you get at 100%. Many formats use a handicap allowance on top: 35/15 in a two player scramble, for example. The result is called the playing handicap. The WHS recommends applying the allowance to the unrounded course handicap and rounding once at the end. In Parmate a Solo Round and a 1vs1 Match use 100%; team formats use the scramble allowance.

Why course rating minus par is in the formula

Since the introduction of the WHS, the difference between course rating and par is part of the course handicap. On a tee that plays harder than its par you get extra strokes, on an easier tee you get fewer. That makes it fair to compare players on different tees, men and women in the same round, and different loops of the same course.

Questions

Is course handicap the same as baanhandicap?

Yes. In the Netherlands the course handicap is also called the baanhandicap. Many clubs show it in a table per tee.

Do women use different numbers?

Yes. Course rating and slope are rated separately for men and women, so use the values for your own tee and gender.
