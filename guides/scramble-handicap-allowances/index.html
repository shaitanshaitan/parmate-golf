Scramble handicap allowances explained

What 35/15, 30/20/10 and 25/20/15/10 mean, why the best player counts most, and when a committee may use something else.

By Robbie, founder of Parmate | Updated 28 November 2026

The short answer

The World Handicap System recommends these allowances for a scramble, applied to each player’s course handicap after sorting from low to high:

  • 2 players: 35% of the lowest, 15% of the highest.
  • 3 players: 30%, 20% and 10%.
  • 4 players: 25%, 20%, 15% and 10%.

The parts are added and the total is rounded once to give the team handicap.

The allowance table

2 players35%15%
3 players30%20%10%
4 players25%20%15%10%

The percentages are recommendations in Appendix C of the Rules of Handicapping. They are designed to give every team the same chance of winning, whatever the mix of handicaps in the team.

Why the lowest handicap gets the biggest share

A scramble team plays the best shot every time. On most holes that is the drive, approach or putt of the strongest player, so the strong player’s ability carries the team far more than the others’. If you simply added up everybody’s handicap, teams with one very good player would get far too many strokes. Weighting the lowest handicap most corrects for that.

What the percentages add up to

A two player team gets 50% of its combined course handicaps in total, a three player team 60% and a four player team 70%. The more players, the more shots to choose from, so each extra player adds a smaller share.

Can a committee use other percentages?

Yes. The allowances are recommendations, and a committee can set its own for a competition, for example a society day where everyone should be in with a chance. In Parmate you pick the WHS values with one tap or set a custom split for each player.

In April 2026 an update to the recommended allowances gave committees more choice for stroke play formats only. It states that no change is made to the match play and scramble allowances, so the table above still applies.

How the allowance is applied

Apply the percentage to the unrounded course handicap and round only the team total. That way there is one rounding instead of several. See the full method in how to calculate a scramble team handicap.

Questions

What is the allowance for a 2 person scramble?

35% of the lower course handicap plus 15% of the higher course handicap, rounded once.

What is the allowance for a 4 person scramble?

25%, 20%, 15% and 10%, from the lowest to the highest course handicap, added up and rounded once.

Does the allowance apply to the handicap index or the course handicap?

To the course handicap. First turn each index into a course handicap for the tee played, then apply the percentage.
