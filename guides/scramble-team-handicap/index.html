How to calculate a scramble team handicap

The World Handicap System way, step by step, with worked examples for teams of two, three and four players.

By Robbie, founder of Parmate | Updated 28 November 2026

The short answer

  1. Work out each player’s course handicap: handicap index × slope ÷ 113 + (course rating − par), using the tee that player plays.
  2. Sort the players from lowest to highest course handicap.
  3. Apply the scramble allowance: 35% and 15% for two players, 30%, 20% and 10% for three, 25%, 20%, 15% and 10% for four.
  4. Add the results and round once to the nearest whole number. That is the team handicap.

Step 1: course handicap for every player

A handicap index says how good a golfer is in general. A course handicap says how many strokes that golfer gets on one particular course from one particular tee. Under the World Handicap System it is:

Course handicap = handicap index × (slope rating ÷ 113) + (course rating − par)

Every player uses the tee they actually play, so a team can mix tees, men and women. Do not round the course handicap yet: the WHS recommends applying the allowance to the unrounded course handicap, so there is only one rounding at the end.

Step 2: sort the team from low to high

The allowance depends on position, not on who is who. The player with the lowest course handicap gets the biggest percentage.

Step 3: apply the scramble allowance

These are the allowances the WHS recommends for a scramble in Appendix C of the Rules of Handicapping:

Team sizeLowest course handicap2nd3rdHighest
2 players35%15%
3 players30%20%10%
4 players25%20%15%10%

A competition committee may choose different percentages, for example for a social day. Parmate uses the WHS values by default and lets you set your own.

Step 4: add up and round once

Add the parts together and round to the nearest whole number. The result is the number of strokes the team gets for the round.

Worked examples

All examples use the same tee: course rating 70.2, slope 122, par 71.

Two players: index 8.2 and 14.5

2.82 + 2.23 = 5.05, so the team handicap is 5.

Three players: index 6.4, 15.0 and 24.8

Course handicaps 6.11, 15.39 and 25.98. With 30%, 20% and 10%: 1.83 + 3.08 + 2.60 = 7.51, so the team handicap is 8.

Four players: index 4.2, 11.6, 18.9 and 30.5

Course handicaps 3.73, 11.72, 19.61 and 32.13. With 25%, 20%, 15% and 10%: 0.93 + 2.34 + 2.94 + 3.21 = 9.43, so the team handicap is 9.

Playing 9 holes

For a 9 hole round, halve the handicap index and use the 9 hole course rating, slope and par: course handicap = (index ÷ 2) × (slope ÷ 113) + (course rating − par). Then apply the allowance in the same way. Example: index 14.5 on a 9 hole loop with rating 33.0, slope 119 and par 34 gives 7.25 × 1.053 − 1 = 6.63.

Common mistakes

  • Averaging the handicap indexes. (8.2 + 14.5) ÷ 2 = 11.35 has nothing to do with the WHS scramble handicap of 5.
  • Using the index instead of the course handicap. Slope, course rating and par change the number, sometimes by several strokes.
  • Rounding each player first. Rounding every part and then the total can move the team handicap by a stroke.
  • Giving the big percentage to the wrong player. The lowest course handicap always gets the largest share.
  • Forgetting plus handicaps. A plus handicap counts as a negative number in the formula.

Questions

Why does the best player count most?

In a scramble the team chooses the best shot every time, and that is usually the shot of the strongest player. Giving the lowest handicap the biggest percentage keeps the match fair between strong and weak teams.

Can players in one team use different tees?

Yes. Each player’s course handicap uses the rating, slope and par of their own tee, so men, women and different tees can play in one team.

Is this the same as a Texas scramble?

A Texas scramble is a scramble with a minimum number of drives per player. The handicap calculation is the same, unless the committee sets its own percentages.
